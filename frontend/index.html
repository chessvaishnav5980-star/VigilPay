              What scam messages really look like

              These are made-up examples based on common police and RBI warnings. Each one has the warning signs marked.

              Unknown number · WhatsApp

              Congratulations! You won ₹5,000 cashback. Scan this QR and enter your UPI PIN to receive it within 10 minutes.

              Why it's a scam: a PIN never receives money, and the deadline is pressure.
              VM-BANKKYC · SMS

              Dear customer, your KYC expires today. Your account will be blocked. Call 98XXXXXXXX now and keep your OTP ready.

              Why it's a scam: banks never ask for an OTP on a call, and use official numbers.
              Support agent · Phone call

              To fix your failed payment, please download AnyDesk and tell me the 9-digit code. Don't tell anyone about this call.

              Why it's a scam: the app lets them control your phone. Secrecy is a classic trick.
              Unknown number · SMS

              Your pending challan of ₹500 will become ₹5,000 tomorrow. Install RTO_Challan.apk to pay now.

              Why it's a scam: a real challan never comes as an app file over chat.
              New number · WhatsApp

              Hi, this is my new number. I'm in the hospital, please send ₹8,000 urgently. I'll explain later. Don't call.

              Why it's a scam: call your relative on their old, saved number first.
              OLX buyer · Chat

              I'll buy your phone today. I've sent a request. Just approve it and enter PIN to get your money.

              Why it's a scam: approving a request pays the buyer. Buyers only need your UPI ID.

              Questions people ask

              Short answers to the doubts we heard most while testing with family and classmates.

              Why does receiving money never need my PIN?

              Your UPI PIN is the key that approves money leaving your account. Money coming in is credited by the bank without any approval from you. If anything asks for your PIN "to receive" money, it is really taking money out.

              Can scanning a QR code give me money?

              No. Scanning a QR code only opens a payment screen to pay someone. Nobody can send you money because you scanned their code.

              What is a "collect request"?

              It is a request that asks you to pay. It appears in your UPI app as "Requested money". A genuine sender never needs you to approve anything for you to receive. If a stranger sends one, decline it and block the sender.

              Someone asked for my OTP. What do I do?

              Hang up and do not share it. If you already shared an OTP, call your bank on the number printed on your card and call 1930 straight away. The sooner you report, the better the chance of stopping the transfer.

              Does a real-looking name or logo mean it's safe?

              No. Names, photos, logos and even caller IDs can be copied. Judge by what you are asked to do: share an OTP, install an app, pay first or hurry up are all warning signs.

              Does VigilPay save what I type?

              No. Numbers that look like a PIN or OTP are removed in your browser before anything is sent. We only keep the result of a check, such as "scam" and a risk percentage, never your words.

              Is VigilPay always right?

              No. It can miss new tricks and it can be wrong. It is a helper, not a guarantee. When in doubt, stop and call your bank using the number on your card or in its official app.

              What if my situation isn't in the scam guide?

              Describe it anyway. VigilPay looks for general red flags too, and the AI can explain unfamiliar situations. If it says it is not clear, answer its question, or phone your bank before paying.

              Where do I report a fraud?

              Call 1930 (the national cybercrime helpline) and file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Also inform your bank so they can block your cards and UPI.

              Words scammers use, in plain language

              UPI PIN
              The secret number that approves a payment from your account. Never tell anyone.
              OTP
              A one-time code sent by SMS. Whoever has it can approve a transaction as you.
              UPI ID (VPA)
              Your payment address, like name@bank. Sharing it is safe. Sharing your PIN is not.
              Collect request
              A message asking you to pay money. Always read who is asking and how much.
              KYC
              Know Your Customer: the ID check banks do. Banks never complete it by asking for your OTP on a call.
              Vishing
              Phishing by voice call: a caller pretends to be the bank, police or a courier.
              Phishing
              A fake message or link made to steal your details or money.
              Screen-sharing app
              Apps like AnyDesk or TeamViewer that let another person see and control your phone.
              APK
              An Android app file. One sent over chat can secretly read your messages and OTPs.
              NPCI
              National Payments Corporation of India, which runs UPI.

              Help your family stay safe

              Grandparents and parents are targeted most. Five minutes of talking can save their savings.

              Agree on a family code word

              If someone calls claiming to be a relative in trouble, ask for the code word before sending money.

              Teach one sentence

              "A PIN is only for paying, never for receiving." Ask them to repeat it, and practise with the demo above.

              Hang up and call back

              Anyone who says they are from the bank can be checked by calling the number printed on the card.

              No remote apps, ever

              Remove AnyDesk or TeamViewer if they are installed. Real support never needs them.

              Tell someone early

              Scammers say "don't tell anyone". Make it normal to say "I'll ask my son or daughter first".

              Save 1930

              Put the helpline in their phone contacts as "Cyber fraud help 1930" so it is easy to find in a panic.
